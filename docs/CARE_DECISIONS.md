# Care choices and service visibility

The live investigation found injured trainers talking instead of requesting care, including Miguel with a fainted Grimer inside a staffed Center. Viridian's assigned nurse was away, and Dev had not requested service. No injured trainer in that sampled save had a pending request. These observations alone do not prove why an individual model selected an action.

Two implementation defects were reproduced: a dense PC menu could remove `heal_party` at the 128-choice boundary, and bulk selling could fill every choice and remove movement. The older live save also lacked the new `workplace` fields, so its assigned staff received empty workplace duty views and no explicit return route.

Care summaries now report an individual's own party injuries, fainting, status and PP, visible healing options and feasible Center journeys. At a Center they report public aggregate staffing and the individual's queue position. Staff see their own existing assignment and a feasible return option even without newer workplace metadata. No biography, memory or private party belonging to another entity is disclosed.

Service, care, a compact range of movement/conversation alternatives, and trade replies survive menu truncation. Selling is paged in groups of ten distinct items so all goods remain accessible without occupying the entire menu. Care journeys use the normal permission/collision-aware planner; disconnected, inaccessible or blocked destinations are not invented.

Provider guidance explains that commitments can be interrupted for care or duties and that speech does not execute healing or service. Rephrased conversations with the same person are recognized as repetition; a notice asks the individual to assess observed replies rather than claiming progress. The model still chooses the action. Observations and route suggestions never heal, move, change goals or queue a request. Tests explicitly preserve a model-selected conversation despite a fainted-party care summary.

These changes fix choice availability and information, rather than impose a healing schedule. Actual autonomous care behavior requires separate live evidence; deterministic tests alone cannot establish it.
